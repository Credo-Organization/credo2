# PRAMAAN Card v2 — Three Directions With an Actual Point of View

---

## 1. What went wrong with v1

The output isn't bad. It's **competent and forgettable**, which is worse — bad gets fixed, forgettable gets scrolled past.

My brief said *"premium fintech credential aesthetic, high-end SaaS."* That is not a point of view. It's what every B2B product on earth is already aiming for. You got exactly what I asked for and what I asked for was generic.

### The three specific clichés in your images

| Cliché | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| **Circuit-board trace pattern** | The single most overused "this is technology" texture. It appears on approximately every hackathon poster ever made. It signals *tech* to nobody, because everyone uses it. |
| **Shield + checkmark** | Generic security iconography. It says antivirus, not credential. |
| **Dark navy + teal glow** | This palette peaked around 2019–2021 in crypto and fintech. It reads dated now, and it reads *corporate*, which is the opposite of a student artifact. |

Image 3 is the weakest of the three: big embossed shield plus chevron stripes is **antivirus box art**. Nothing about it says "a student's verified skills."

### The real diagnosis

Codolio's card works because it has **one bold idea** — *your competitive programming stats, as a flex.* Everything on it serves that idea.

Your card is trying to look trustworthy. That's a quality, not an idea. Nothing on it is ownable.

**But you have an obvious ownable idea sitting in your product name.**

It's called a **PASSPORT**. Passports have stamps, seals, guilloché line-work, holographic foil, and machine-readable zones. That is a rich, instantly-recognisable visual language, it is thematically exact, and **not one other team in that competition will use it.**

---

## 2. The audience tension — read this before you pick

There are two different audiences and they want opposite things:

- **The SIH panel** — often faculty and government-adjacent. Wants credible, serious, institutional.
- **Students** — the actual users. Want something they'd screenshot and post.

**Direction A threads both.** Directions B and C lean harder toward students and carry more risk with a formal panel.

My recommendation: **build A as the hero image for the deck.** If you want a second image showing product-in-app, use B.

# DIRECTION A — THE PASSPORT ★ recommended

Real travel-document visual language, executed seriously. Distinctive, thematic, and credible to a formal panel.

**Key devices:** guilloché security line-work · embossed intaglio seal · holographic foil patch · rubber-stamp skill marks · machine-readable zone.

```
An official-looking digital credential document rendered as a premium 
passport-style card, portrait orientation, vertical ID proportions, shown 
flat and straight-on with no perspective tilt, on a deep charcoal background 
with a soft even shadow.

SURFACE: rich ink-navy base (#131A3A) with an intricate guilloché security 
pattern — fine interwoven curved line-work like on a banknote or passport 
page — rendered in a barely-visible lighter navy at 12% opacity across the 
whole surface. A subtle paper-fibre texture and light film grain overlay. 
Along the right edge, a narrow vertical holographic foil strip with an iridescent 
teal-to-violet-to-gold shift.

TOP ZONE: the word "PRAMAAN" in a refined serif-adjacent uppercase, warm 
off-white, generously letter-spaced, upper left. Below it in small 
letter-spaced uppercase warm gold: "SKILL PASSPORT". Upper right, an embossed 
circular intaglio seal — a raised medallion with fine concentric engraved 
line-work and a small emblem at its centre, rendered in warm gold with soft 
relief shadowing, looking pressed into the surface rather than printed on it.

IDENTITY ZONE: a thin gold hairline rule, then on the left a small rounded 
square containing the initials "AK" in warm off-white on deep navy, and to its 
right two lines of text — a name in off-white, and beneath it a small 
monospace identifier line in muted gold.

STAMP ZONE — the hero of the card: four overlapping rubber-stamp impressions 
scattered at natural irregular angles between minus 12 and plus 15 degrees, 
with authentic ink-bleed texture and slightly broken uneven edges, as if 
pressed by hand at different times. Each stamp is a rounded rectangle outline 
containing a short word and a tiny date beneath. Two stamps in deep emerald 
ink, one in warm amber ink, one in muted slate. The stamps overlap the 
guilloché pattern and each other slightly.

TIER RULE: a thin horizontal band divided into six small connected segments, 
ramping left to right from muted slate through soft teal to strong emerald, 
with tiny tick marks beneath. No text inside.

MACHINE-READABLE ZONE at the bottom: two lines of monospace characters in 
off-white on a slightly lighter navy band, in the style of a passport MRZ, 
with angle-bracket filler characters. Small, precise, technical.

BOTTOM-LEFT: a crisp white QR code on a small warm-off-white tile with a thin 
gold border, positioned beside the MRZ band.

STYLE: official document design, banknote-engraving craftsmanship, restrained 
and institutional, warm gold and emerald against deep ink navy, tactile and 
printed rather than digital-glossy, editorial precision, sharp focus, 4K.

AVOID: circuit-board patterns, shield icons, checkmark badges, glass or 
glossy 3D reflections, perspective tilt, neon glow, teal-on-black tech 
styling, photographs of people, cartoon style, gradients that look like 
software UI.
```

**Why this wins:** it's the only direction where the visual language *is* the product name. A judge sees it and understands "passport" before reading a word. And "we designed it as an actual travel document because that's the mental model — portable, stamped by authorities, you carry it between borders" is a strong 10-second answer.

# DIRECTION B — THE BENTO

The most current-feeling of the three. Flat, bold, high-contrast, single accent. This is what 2026 product design actually looks like — bento grids, oversized numerals, monospace labels, grain.

```
A modern digital credential card, portrait orientation, vertical proportions, 
shown completely flat and straight-on with no tilt, no 3D perspective, no 
shadow, on a pure black background.

SURFACE: true black (#0A0A0A) with a fine film-grain noise texture across the 
entire surface. A single electric acid-lime accent colour (#CCFF00) used 
sparingly and deliberately. All other elements in off-white and mid-grey. No 
gradients anywhere.

LAYOUT: an asymmetric bento grid of rounded rectangles of deliberately unequal 
sizes, separated by generous black gutters, filling the card edge to edge.

TOP-LEFT CELL, wide and short: the word "PRAMAAN" in a heavy geometric 
condensed sans-serif, off-white, very large relative to the cell, tightly 
tracked. Beneath it in tiny uppercase monospace acid-lime: "SKILL PASSPORT".

TOP-RIGHT CELL, small square: filled solid acid-lime, containing a single 
minimal black glyph — a simple abstract mark, not a shield or checkmark.

LARGE HERO CELL, spanning most of the width: dark charcoal fill with a thin 
grey border, containing one enormous numeral "3" in off-white taking up most 
of the cell height, and a small uppercase monospace label in acid-lime at the 
bottom edge reading "ISSUER-VERIFIED".

TWO MEDIUM CELLS side by side beneath: each dark charcoal with a thin border, 
each containing a large off-white numeral — "7" and "2" — with small 
uppercase monospace grey labels beneath.

WIDE THIN CELL: six connected horizontal segments ramping from dark grey 
through mid-grey to full acid-lime, left to right. No text.

SKILL CELL: a cluster of small rounded outline chips with thin grey borders, 
containing short uppercase monospace words, arranged in two irregular rows.

BOTTOM CELL, wide: black fill, with a crisp white QR code on the left and to 
its right two short lines of small uppercase monospace grey text.

STYLE: contemporary flat UI design, bento grid layout, brutalist-adjacent 
restraint, oversized display typography, monospace for all small labels, heavy 
use of negative space, single accent colour discipline, film grain texture, 
crisp vector edges, 4K.

AVOID: 3D rendering, tilt, perspective, drop shadows, glass reflections, 
gradients, circuit patterns, shield icons, glow effects, more than one accent 
colour, photographs, decorative illustration.
```

**Risk:** genuinely current, and a faculty panel may read acid-lime brutalism as unserious. Use it as an in-app screenshot, not as the credential hero.

# DIRECTION C — THE VERIFICATION RECEIPT

The most dev-authentic. Looks like a printed verification slip from a terminal. Students who code will love it; non-technical judges may not get it.

```
A digital credential rendered as a verification receipt or printed terminal 
slip, portrait orientation, tall narrow proportions, shown flat and 
straight-on on a dark charcoal background with a soft shadow.

SURFACE: near-black (#0D0D0D) with subtle horizontal scanline texture at very 
low opacity. All typography is monospace. Accent colour is a soft phosphor 
green (#4ADE80) used for status markers only; all body text in warm off-white 
and mid-grey.

TOP: the word "PRAMAAN" in monospace uppercase, off-white, with a thin 
horizontal dotted rule beneath it running the full width. Under the rule, 
small grey monospace text reading "SKILL PASSPORT".

HEADER BLOCK: two lines of small monospace text in a label-and-value 
arrangement, left-aligned labels in grey, right-aligned values in off-white, 
separated by rows of dot leaders.

STATUS BLOCK: three lines, each beginning with a small phosphor-green filled 
square marker, followed by a monospace count and an uppercase label. Between 
each line, a thin dotted separator rule.

TIER LINE: a single row of six small square blocks, ramping from dark grey to 
full phosphor green left to right, with tiny monospace tick labels beneath.

SKILL BLOCK: short uppercase monospace words in a simple left-aligned list, 
each preceded by a small bracket marker.

DIVIDER: a full-width row of repeating dash characters, like a receipt tear line.

FOOTER: a crisp white QR code centred, with two lines of tiny monospace grey 
text beneath it, and below that a row of characters resembling a checksum 
string.

BOTTOM EDGE: a torn-paper receipt edge with a subtle irregular zigzag profile.

STYLE: terminal aesthetic, thermal-receipt design, monospace typography 
throughout, high contrast, extremely restrained, functional rather than 
decorative, no ornament, sharp focus, 4K.

AVOID: colour beyond the single green accent, 3D rendering, tilt, glass, glow, 
gradients, circuit patterns, shield icons, illustration, photographs, 
decorative flourishes.
```

## 3. Rules that apply to all three

**Status, not counts.** Still true, still the most important content rule. `3 Issuer-verified` — never `23 repositories`.

**No photo.** Initials only. It keeps appearance out of a recruiter-facing artifact, which is consistent with everything else you built.

**Generate flat, not tilted.** All three prompts specify straight-on. The 3D tilt in v1 is what made it look like a stock template — every AI product mockup has that tilt. Flat looks deliberate.

**Six words maximum of rendered text if you can help it.** You've had two character errors already. Where possible, generate the shell and overlay real text.

**The clichés to keep out, permanently:** circuit-board traces, shield-with-checkmark, glass reflection sweeps, neon glow, navy-and-teal.

---

## 4. Honest read

Direction A is the one I'd build. Not because it's the trendiest — B is — but because it's the only one where **the design argues your point for you.** A passport is portable, it's stamped by authorities rather than self-declared, and it travels across borders. That is your entire product thesis expressed as an object.

B is the best-looking by 2026 standards and the riskiest with your specific judges.

C is the most likeable to students who code and the most likely to lose a non-technical panel.

Generate A first. If it comes out weak after three or four attempts, the concept isn't wrong — the prompt needs tightening around the stamps, because that's the element carrying the whole idea.
